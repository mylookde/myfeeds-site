    01

    Build your own online shop in minutes

    Minutes, not a project. No theme to buy, no developer, and no second website to keep running.

    One

    Paste your product feed URL

    Your affiliate network gives you a product feed: one file with every product it carries, prices and stock included. Paste its URL. MyFeeds reads it, and the products are on your site.

    Two

    Name your categories

    Clothing in this example, so Men, Women, Shoes. Put products in yourself, or let MyFeeds propose them and tick off what fits.

    Three

    Turn your shop on

    Pick the address it lives at. The shop goes live as a page on your own site, with its own sitemap for Google.

    02

    What your shop looks like in the WordPress admin

    Everything above is what your reader meets. This is where you steer it.

    You sort products into categories

    Name the categories your shop should have. Clothing in this example, so Men, Women, Shoes. A category can sit inside another one, so Men can hold Jackets, and Jackets can hold Rain Jackets. A car parts site would say Brakes, Filters, Tyres.

    Nothing moves without you. Search your catalogue and put single products into a category yourself, one by one. Or let MyFeeds propose them by keyword, brand and feed and tick off the ones that fit. Only if you then turn rules on does a later import sort itself, and even then the matches wait for your approval.

    Every category is a page with its own address, its own heading and your own text above the products. Its title and description for Google are yours to set, on one category or on many at once. Rename a category and the old address forwards to the new one, so nothing you had linked breaks.

    A shortlist inside a category

    Jackets holds every jacket you have, all year round. What you want to point at in October is a handful of them. A style is that handful: you pick products out of one category and give the set a name. Fall Jackets inside Jackets, in this example. A car parts site might keep Winter Tyres inside Tyres.

    "Style" is only the default word, and you can rename it to anything that fits what you write about: Looks, Collections, Editions, Seasons.

    Your readers meet it in two places. It is a page of its own, with its own address, your own words on it and its own title and description for Google, so it can be what you link to from a post. And it is a box in the filter list, so someone already looking at Jackets can switch it on and stay where they are.

    You can pick every product by hand, or set rules like a brand, a colour, a price range or a word in the product name, and let MyFeeds collect the matches out of every later import. They wait on a list until you wave them through, so nothing turns up in your shop that you have not seen.

    Off until you switch it on in the shop settings. A style can also stay out of the shop and be there only for you, when you place products in a post.

    yourblog.com/shop/men/jackets/style/fall-jackets/

    Make it look like your own

    Set the colours, fonts, how many products sit in a row, what happens when someone points at a product, which side the filters are on, how big the pictures are and much more. Products per row is set separately for phone, tablet and laptop, so a phone is not a squeezed laptop.

    A live preview sits next to the controls and follows every change as you make it, and it switches between laptop, tablet and phone. What you see there is the same card your visitors get, built by the same code.

    No theme to buy and no code anywhere. You can put everything back to the defaults, and undo that too if you change your mind.

    You see which products earn

    Clicks per post, product and brand, how many people came, and the commissions your networks confirm, in one place instead of one login per network.

    Every link out goes through your own site first, so the click is counted before the reader leaves. It keeps counting even when a visitor blocks scripts, which is where most click counters go quiet.

    Products that have gone inactive, because the merchant stopped listing them, are flagged here too, together with the posts that still link to them, so you know what to fix first.

    Before you start the trial

    The questions people ask about the shop.

    What is the shop exactly?

    A real shop page on your own site. Your products sit in categories, visitors narrow them down with filters, search them and sort them, and every buy happens at the merchant through your affiliate link. You never take a payment or send a parcel.

    Do I have to sort every product by hand?

    No, and you are never forced into it either. Put single products into a category yourself, or let MyFeeds propose them by keyword, brand and feed and tick off what fits. If you then turn rules on, later imports sort themselves, and those matches wait on a list for your approval.

    Does the shop change my blog?

    No. The shop has its own address, which you choose, and your posts stay untouched. Both draw from the same catalogue, so a product is there for whichever of the two you build first.

    Can I make the shop look like my site?

    Yes. Colours, fonts, products per row, the size and shape of the pictures, what happens when someone points at a product, which side the filters are on, how the pages are numbered and much more, all with a live preview next to the controls. No code anywhere.

    What can I put in it?

    Anything your affiliate networks hand you as a feed: fashion, tech, home, beauty, garden, hobbies, parts. The plugin does not care what the subject is. If your network gives you a product feed URL, you can build a shop from it.

    What are styles, and do I need them?

    A style is a shorter, hand-picked list inside one category: Fall Jackets inside Jackets, in this example. It gets a page of its own and a box in the filters, so readers can switch it on where they already are. It is off until you switch it on, and a shop works perfectly well without it.

    How long is the trial?

    14 days with everything in it: the shop, the design editor, the SEO tools and the rules that sort your products. Cancel from your dashboard whenever you like. Anything you have already built stays as it is.

    What happens to my things if I cancel?

    The shop page stops being reachable for visitors. Your products, feeds, posts and the free plugin keep working. Subscribing again brings the shop back the way you left it.
