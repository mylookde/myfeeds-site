Guides · Product feeds

How to get a shop's product feed into WordPress.

For bloggers with affiliate links: your affiliate network gives you the product list of a shop you're an affiliate for, as a link or a file. A plugin reads it, and you pick products from it inside WordPress.

The short version

Three steps and three facts, in case that is all you came for.

  1. Get the feed link. Your affiliate network has it, for each shop that has accepted you. Part 3 shows where.
  2. Give it to a plugin. WordPress cannot read a feed by itself. Part 5 helps you choose.
  3. Pick products in a post. Your readers see them with picture and price.

In short

  • A product feed is a shop's whole range as one list. One row per product: name, price, picture and your affiliate link.
  • You need one when a post shows products. For a link inside a sentence, copy it from your network.
  • Take the link when you can get one. A link can be read again later. A file stays as it was on the day you saved it.

Part 1 of 8

Do you need a product feed at all?

Often, no. Here is how to tell before you spend an evening on it.

A shop has accepted you into its affiliate programme, and somewhere in your network's menu you have seen the words “product feed”. It sounds useful. It also sounds like work.

A feed helps as soon as a post shows the product itself, with its picture or its price. For a link inside a sentence, copying the link from your network is enough.

A feed helps

When the post shows products

  • A roundup or gift guide with prices. A price typed by hand has to be checked again. A feed carries the shop's current price.
  • Many posts, the same few shops. Past a few dozen posts, checking them all by hand stops happening.
  • A few products, shown with picture and price. Your picks can follow the shop's price and link.
You can skip it

When a link will do

  • One or two links in a post. Copy the link from your network and paste it.
  • A page of discount codes. A product feed lists products, not vouchers.
  • You hope it brings new readers. It gives you products to show the readers you have.

Selling your own products? You need the opposite direction, a feed that sends your range to Google or a marketplace. That is a different guide.

Is it worth it for a small site?

AWIN, one of the large networks, says feeds are mainly used by price comparison sites, shopping portals, review sites and deal platforms, but that any publisher may use them. A blog uses a small part of the list, the handful of products it would have linked anyway.

So the size of your site does not decide it. If you recommend six products a year and stand behind each one, a wrong price on any of them costs you a reader's trust.

Why AWIN comes up so often. Its help pages are public, so we can link what we quote. Part 3 shows where other networks differ.

Part 2 of 8

What is in a product feed?

One row for each product, one column for each thing the shop knows about it.

Every online shop keeps a list of what it sells. Many share it with their affiliates in a form a program can read. Think of a very long spreadsheet. Here is one row, turned on its side so it fits your screen.

One example row of a product feed, column by column
Column in this feedWhat this row says
aw_product_idWhich product: 31415926
product_nameName: Court High-Top Leather Black
search_pricePrice today: 96.00
product_price_oldOld price: 129.00
merchant_image_urlPicture: https://…/court-high-top.jpg
aw_deep_linkYour link: https://… to this exact product
in_stockIn stock: 1
merchant_nameShop: Kicks Corner

An invented row with the column names of AWIN's classic format. Other networks name them differently. Example product, sample prices.

What one row teaches you

  • Names differ. This feed calls the price search_price, a feed in Google's shopping format calls it price. Matching them up is called mapping, and your plugin does it.
  • An empty column means the shop left it out. No plugin can show what the feed does not carry.
  • One column pays you. aw_deep_link is your affiliate link for exactly this product. Its neighbour merchant_deep_link is the shop's plain address, which credits nobody.

Part 3 of 8

Where do you find yours?

Each network keeps it somewhere else and calls it something else. One worked example, then what the others give you.

Look for the part of your account where you choose a shop's product feed and get a download address for it. Networks call it a product feed, a data feed or a product catalog, and they rename their menus.

Two things have to be true first. The shop has accepted you, and it offers a feed at all. Not every shop does.

A worked example: AWIN

As of October 2026 AWIN calls the tool Create-a-Feed. It sits in the Toolbox menu, under Links & Tools.

  1. Choose the language first

    Create-a-Feed only lists feeds in the language you pick. If a shop seems to be missing, set it to Any.

  2. Pick the shop's feed

    Most shops have one, called Default. Some have one for each part of their range.

  3. Tick the columns

    Choose Select All, or at least make sure the tracked link, aw_deep_link, is among them. A sale through the shop's plain address is not credited to you.

  4. Copy the address

    AWIN calls this a reusable download link. Copy it.

    What you copied looks roughly like this

    https://…/apikey/YOUR-KEY/fid/12345/format/csv/…

    • YOUR-KEY is a key for feeds only, not your publisher API key. Treat the whole address like a password.
    • 12345 is the number of the shop's feed. Another shop, another number.

    A shortened example. Your address is longer.

Just joined the shop? AWIN says it can take up to an hour to appear. Whether a shop has a feed is shown in your list of programmes, in the column Product Feed.

Part 4 of 8

A link or a file? Take the link when you can get one.

Both hold the same rows on the day you get them. The difference shows a month later.

A link is a web address your plugin reads the feed from. It often carries a personal key, so treat it like a password. A file is the same list, saved on one day.

If your network only sends files

Then the file is your feed, and keeping it fresh is a job you do by hand. Upload a new one whenever prices matter to a post, for example before a sale or once a month.

If that is too much, some plugins get the products through an API instead, which is one program asking another directly. Datafeedr and Content Egg in part 5 work that way, for the networks on their lists.

Part 5 of 8

How does it get into WordPress?

Through a plugin. Which kind depends on what you want your readers to see.

An import is not a page yet

This is where many people get stuck. In one WordPress support thread, someone imports three products, the plugin confirms they are in its data store, and they cannot work out how to get them onto the site. The thread never got an answer.

The likely explanation is a common one: an import only fills a table in your database. Products appear once a second step puts them in a post or on shop pages. So decide where you want them first.

A feed is not a shop. A shop of your own is a bigger project, with its own guide: how to build an affiliate shop in WordPress.

Part 6 of 8

How do you use a feed without trouble from Google?

A feed makes it easy to publish a thousand products. Google has a name for sites that do only that.

Google's spam policies call it thin affiliation. They mean pages with affiliate links whose descriptions and reviews are copied from the shop, with nothing of your own added. A page for each row of a feed, with the shop's description on it, is exactly that.

Affiliate links are not the problem in themselves. The same policies say that taking part in an affiliate programme does not make a site thin. What counts is what you add.

What that means for one post

  • Pick a handful. The feed is a list to choose from. Nobody needs a page for every row.
  • Say why, in your own words. A short review, with a comparison where it helps.

A post like that is much closer to what the policies list as adding value. Nobody can promise how Google rates a single page.

And are you allowed to show all of it?

Mostly yes, with three things to do first. A feed does none of them for you.

  1. Say that you earn a commission. The US Federal Trade Commission wants that close to the recommendation, and says the words “affiliate link” alone, or a “buy now” button, are not enough. Our example, not the FTC's wording: “I earn a commission if you buy through the links in this post.” Outside the US, check your own regulator.
  2. Mark your affiliate links as paid. Google prefers rel="sponsored" and still accepts rel="nofollow". For a link you set by hand, edit it in WordPress, open Advanced and turn on Mark as nofollow. Links a plugin creates should carry the mark already. To check one on a computer, right-click it on the published page and choose Inspect.
  3. Read the programme terms on pictures and texts. The shop and its network set what you may do with them, and we could not confirm one rule that covers them all.

Part 7 of 8

How do prices stay right after you publish?

A new price reaches your page in four steps. It can be late at each of them.

The month after you publish is the reason to bother with a feed at all. Shops change prices, sell out and drop products, and a post that was right in March can be wrong by June.

The shop and the network

How fresh the feed is in the first place is up to the shop. AWIN lets shops refresh a feed up to four times a day or once a week, and recommends at least every 24 hours.

You can check. In its Create-a-Feed tool, the column Last Update shows when a shop last refreshed its feed. If that is weeks ago, its help sends you to the shop's account manager.

Your site

This is the part you control. A link is only read again if your plugin goes back for it on a schedule. AWIN's own WordPress plugin, for one, does not. Its help says files have to be uploaded by hand.

WordPress looks for due tasks only when somebody loads a page, so on a quiet site a nightly job can run late. If that matters, ask your host to run WordPress's scheduled tasks on a timer. Hosts call that a server cron job.

Your page

If a caching plugin or your host saves copies of your pages, visitors see the saved copy until it is renewed. If a price looks old, clear the cache and look again.

Products that disappear

Harder than a new price is a product that leaves the shop for good. The feed stops listing it, and what happens next depends on your plugin.

Every dead link is a commission you earned and never received.

What that costs an old post is its own guide: why affiliate posts stop earning.

How our own plugin does it

Every night, MyFeeds reads a feed link again and updates price, old price, stock, picture and link for the products in your published posts. Once a week it reads each whole feed. That is when new products become searchable and products that have left the feed are noticed.

When a product has left the feed, its card is left out of the post. No grey box takes its place. Until the next weekly run, the card can stay up with its last price. All of this runs on WordPress's own schedule, so on a quiet site it can run late.

You can try this on one post with the free plugin. See the install guide.

Who to ask

What is missing or wrong inside the feed is the shop's to fix, so ask the shop or its account manager. What happens after the list reaches WordPress is a question for the plugin's support.

Start small

One network, one shop, one post. Put three products from a feed into a post you would have written anyway, and look at it again in a month. If the prices still match the shop, add the second shop.

Questions people ask

From WordPress support forums and the networks' own help. The short answer comes first.

Is a product feed the same as a shop?

No. A feed is a list. What appears on your site depends on the plugin that reads it. Part 5 compares the kinds.

Do I need WooCommerce to show affiliate products?

Not for products inside posts. You need it only if your plugin turns the rows of a feed into WooCommerce products, as some shop builders do.

Can I use a feed only to keep prices up to date?

Yes, and it is the best reason to use one. It needs a feed that arrives as a link and a plugin that reads it again on a schedule. Part 7 shows where a price can still be late.

Can I add my own margin to the prices in a feed?

No. As an affiliate you do not sell anything. The shop sets the price and makes the sale, and you earn a commission on it.

Does Amazon have a product feed?

No. Amazon does not offer its associates a product feed file. Product data comes through its Creators API, which Amazon opens to associates with ten qualifying sales in the past 30 days (as of October 2026).

And two shorter ones

Can I get only part of a shop's feed?

Sometimes. AWIN's Create-a-Feed can limit a feed to certain brands, and some shops offer a separate feed for each part of their range. A smaller feed also imports faster.

I can download the feed of a shop I never joined. Does a sale still count?

With AWIN, sometimes. For selected shops that accept affiliates automatically, sales made through the feed are credited before you have joined. That does not apply to all shops, so joining properly is the safe way.
