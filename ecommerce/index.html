What stays buried without one

You already have the products. Without a shop, your readers never get to see most of them.

One

Your best products sit in old posts

A product you wrote about last spring can only be found by someone who finds that one article again. Everything else you imported was never on your site at all.

Two

Readers can read, but not browse

There is no way to narrow things down by colour or price, and no way to see what else you have. Someone who came ready to buy runs out of things to click and leaves.

Three

Shoppers search for pages you do not have

People type a kind of product into Google, not the title of your roundup. With no page for that kind of product, you have nothing for Google to show them.

The products are already inside your WordPress. Everything you imported sits in your own site, waiting. The shop is simply the page that lets people walk through it, filled from the same feeds that already fill your posts.

02

Your side of the same shop

Everything above is what your reader meets. This is where you steer it, in your own WordPress admin.

You sort products into sections

Name the sections your shop should have. Clothing in this example, so Men, Women, Shoes. A car parts site would say Brakes, Filters, Tyres. A section can sit inside another one, so Men can hold Jackets, and Jackets can hold Rain Jackets.

MyFeeds proposes products for each section by keyword, brand and feed, and you tick off what belongs. What you tick teaches it the rule, so products from later imports land in the same sections on their own.

Every section is a page with its own address, its own heading and your own text above the products. Rename one and the old address forwards to the new one, so nothing you had linked breaks.

A shortlist inside a section

Jackets holds everything you carry all year. What you want to point at in October is a handful of them. A style is that handful: you pick products out of one section and give the set a name. Fall Jackets inside Jackets, in this example. A car parts site might keep Winter Tyres inside Tyres.

Your readers meet it in two places. It is a page of its own, with its own address and your own words on it, so it can be what you link to from a post and what Google shows. And it is a box in the filter list, so someone already looking at Jackets can switch it on and stay where they are.

You can pick every product by hand, or set rules like a brand, a colour, a price range or a word in the product name, and let MyFeeds collect the matches out of every later import. They wait on a list until you wave them through, so nothing turns up in your shop that you have not seen.

Off until you switch it on, and you choose the word for it: Styles, Looks, Collections, whatever fits what you write about. A style can also stay out of the shop and be there only for you, when you place products in a post.

03

Google treats it like a shop

The part most affiliate setups never get right. Here it comes with the shop instead of being added later.

  • Google sees productsEach section page carries the extra information search engines read on shop pages: the products on it, their prices, their brands, and where the page sits inside your shop. Those sets of data have names you may meet elsewhere: CollectionPage, BreadcrumbList and ItemList.
  • Addresses that holdSections sit inside each other in the address, one address per page, and a rename forwards the old one for good. Links you or anyone else has already shared keep working.
  • Your own titles and textOne pattern for the whole shop, changeable on any single section, and your own words above and below the products. Nothing is written for you and left that way.
  • Its own sitemapThe shop and its sections go into a sitemap of their own that keeps itself up to date. You hand it to Google once.
  • Page two is a real pageLater pages of a long section are ordinary links with the products in them, so nothing you sell sits behind a button a search engine will not press.

How it fits your site

The shop is a page on your own WordPress site, at an address you choose. It uses the same imported products your posts already use, so there is one set of products and two ways to meet them: inside an article, or by browsing.

Nothing runs through us. The products sit in your own site, your own site serves the pages, and every click out carries your affiliate link.

Before you start the trial

The questions people ask about the shop.

What is the shop exactly?

A real shop page on your own site. Your products sit in sections, visitors narrow them down with filters, search them and sort them, and every buy happens at the merchant through your affiliate link. You never take a payment or send a parcel.

Do I have to sort every product by hand?

No. MyFeeds proposes products for each section by keyword, brand and feed, and you tick off what belongs. That teaches it the rule, so the next import sorts itself and only shows you what it is unsure about.

Does the shop change my blog?

No. The shop has its own address, which you choose, and your posts stay untouched. The same products fill both, so a product you add for a post is already there when you build the shop.

Can I make the shop look like my site?

Yes. Colours, fonts, products per row, the size and shape of the pictures, what a hover does, which side the filters are on, how the pages are numbered and much more, all with a live preview next to the controls. No code anywhere.

What can I put in it?

Anything your affiliate networks hand you as a feed: fashion, tech, home, beauty, garden, hobbies, parts. The plugin does not care what the subject is. If your network gives you a feed address, you can build a shop from it.

What are styles, and do I need them?

A style is a shorter, hand-picked list inside one section: Fall Jackets inside Jackets, in this example. It gets a page of its own and a box in the filters, so readers can switch it on where they already are. It is off until you switch it on, and a shop works perfectly well without it.

How long is the trial?

14 days with everything in it: the shop, the design editor, the SEO work and the sorting help. Cancel from your dashboard whenever you like. Your free setup and the posts you have already built stay as they are.

What happens to my things if I cancel?

The shop page stops being reachable for visitors. Your products, feeds, posts and the free plugin keep working. Subscribing again brings the shop back the way you left it.
