Affiliate shop on WordPress: do you need one?

An affiliate shop is a page on your blog where readers browse the products you recommend and buy them at the shops you link to. You need one when readers come to browse, and you can start small: 50 products you already write about, with or without WooCommerce.

The short answer

In case that is all you came for.

Yes, if readers come to you to browse: for ideas, for what is new, for everything you recommend in one place.

If they arrive at one post, read it and click, product cards inside that post do the job with far less work.

In short

  • You can start with 50 products. The ones you already recommend, in a few categories.
  • You do not need WooCommerce. It is one of four ways, compared in part 3.
  • Filling the shop is the smaller job. Prices, stock and links have to stay right for as long as the page is up.
  • Affiliate links do not make a site spam. Google's spam policies name pages that copy a shop's descriptions and add nothing of their own.

Part 1 of 9

What is an affiliate shop, and what is it not?

It looks like a shop. The selling happens somewhere else.

To a reader it looks like any online shop: categories, a search box, filters, products with picture and price. The difference is the last step. A click on a product opens the shop that sells it, through your affiliate link.

Two things share this name. An “affiliate store” can also mean a shop that pays other people a commission for selling its products. That takes different software. In this guide, you recommend and another shop sells.

Part 2 of 9

Do you need a shop at all?

Five questions and a rule of thumb.

People open a post to read it. They open a shop page to look around. So the test is whether your readers would come to browse.

Tick what is true for you

The ticks are only for you. Nothing is saved.

Three ticks or more

A shop fits

A follower taps the link in your profile, opens a category and finds everything you would pick.

Two or fewer

Cards in your posts are enough

If readers arrive at a handful of posts, read and click, show the products inside those posts. The feed guide shows how. Add a shop the day readers ask where to find everything.

Does a shop earn more than posts?

We found no independent comparison of the two. The figures in circulation come from companies that sell shop software, so start small and let your own readers decide.

If you take the WooCommerce way

WooCommerce has a product type for this, called External/Affiliate product. Its button leads to the shop that sells the product.

The importer under Products, All Products, Import creates such products from a CSV file. Run it again with Update existing products ticked and it overwrites the rows it finds by ID or SKU, so give every product an SKU.

Check any plugin before you build on it. Its WordPress.org page shows when it was last updated and whether support questions get answers.

Part 4 of 9

How do 400 products get in without typing them?

From the shops' own product lists, in four steps.

A shop you're an affiliate for keeps a list of everything it sells: name, price, picture, stock and your affiliate link for each product. That is its product feed, and your affiliate network passes it on as a link or a file. Not every shop offers one.

  1. Get each shop's product feed

    Take the link if there is one. A link can be read again later, and a file stays as it was. The feed guide shows where each network keeps it.

  2. Give it to the tool from part 3

    A feed plugin takes the link or the file. WooCommerce's importer takes a CSV file in which Type says external, External URL holds your affiliate link and Button text the words on the button.

    A feed plugin's import only fills a list to choose from. Nothing shows until you put products on a page.

  3. Name your categories

    Use the words a reader would look for. Clothing in this example: Jackets, Shoes, Bags. A garden blog would say Tools, Seeds, Pots.

  4. Choose what goes in

    A feed holds the shop's whole range. Start with the products your posts already link to, then add only what you would recommend.

Amazon is the exception. It hands out a product feed only with written approval. Its product data comes through the Creators API, open to associates with ten qualifying sales in the past 30 days (as of October 2026).

Part 5 of 9

Will Google punish a shop built from a feed? That depends on what you add.

Google's spam policies, last updated in August 2026, name two things a feed makes easy.

  • Thin affiliation. Pages with affiliate links where descriptions and reviews are copied from the shop and nothing of your own is added.
  • Scaled content abuse. Many pages made mainly to rank. One of Google's examples: pages generated from feeds in bulk, with little on them for the reader.

The same page says that not every site in an affiliate programme is thin.

Google's examples, in our words

What a good affiliate site adds

  • Its own product reviews.
  • Testing and ratings.
  • Product comparisons.
  • Extra information about prices.
  • A way to browse products or categories.

A shop that helps readers browse by category is on that list. A thousand pages that each repeat one row of a feed are what the two policies describe.

After Google's spam update of August 2026, the consultant Glenn Gabe described an Amazon affiliate site built entirely from Amazon's product data. By his count, it lost its place for more than 14,000 search terms.

What belongs in Google's index?

Our suggestion, by kind of page. Where Google has advice of its own, the row says so.

Kinds of page in an affiliate shop, and whether to let Google index them
Kind of pageWhat we would do
Shop front, categories and hand-picked collectionsIndex. Add a few sentences of your own: what is in here, how you chose it.
Page 2 and onward of a categoryLeave open. Google's advice: each page keeps its own address and is not pointed back at page 1.
Filtered, sorted and search viewsKeep out. The same products in endless combinations. Google names two ways: a noindex tag, or a robots.txt rule.
One page for each product, with the shop's descriptionNoindex, or go without. This is the page the thin affiliation wording describes.

One way for each page, not both. A noindex tag belongs in the head of the page, and Google reads it only if it may crawl that page. Block the page in robots.txt and the tag is never seen.

Nobody can promise how Google rates a single page. What you control is whether your category page says something the shop's own page does not.

Part 6 of 9

Who keeps the prices right after launch?

A page with prices can be wrong in a way a text link cannot.

The shops you link to change prices, sell out and add products all the time. AWIN, one of the large networks, says its shops often update their feeds several times a day.

Your page only follows if the feed is read again: by you, each time you repeat an import, or by a tool on a schedule. The table in part 3 says which.

What to recommend is your judgement. Whether today's price is right should not be your job.

WordPress looks for due tasks only when somebody loads a page, so on a quiet site a nightly update can run late. If that matters, ask your host for a server cron job, which runs them on a timer.

Part 7 of 9

Are you allowed to show other shops' products?

Yes, with four things in place. As of October 2026.

Four rules for an affiliate shop page
The ruleWhat to do
Say that you earn a commissionThe US Federal Trade Commission wants that clear and close to the recommendation. “Affiliate link” alone, or a “buy now” button, does not do it. Our reading for a shop page: a sentence above the products, such as “I earn a commission when you buy through the links on this page.”
Add Amazon's sentenceIf you link to Amazon, its operating agreement asks for this, clearly and prominently: “As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.”
Mark affiliate links as paidGoogle prefers rel="sponsored" and accepts rel="nofollow". To see what your plugin sets, right-click a product link on a computer and choose Inspect.
Use pictures and prices as the programme allowsAmazon: prices only through its API or in a link Amazon serves. Pictures through the API, never saved on your server. Other shops: each programme's terms decide. We could not confirm one rule that covers them all.

Outside the US, check your own regulator.

Part 8 of 9

How do you start, and how do you know it works?

With 50 products and four weeks.

  1. Week 1: choose 50 products

    Take the ones you already recommend and sort them into three to five categories. Write two or three sentences of your own for each category.

  2. Week 2: publish and link it

    Put the shop page in your menu and the commission sentence above the products. Link to it from your most-read posts.

  3. Week 3: send readers there

    One email, a few pins, the link in your profile. Do not wait for search to find the page.

  4. Week 4: read three numbers

    Visits to the shop page, from your analytics. Clicks and commission for each shop, from your affiliate network. And in Search Console, whether your category pages are indexed or wait under Crawled - currently not indexed.

    Your network counts posts and shop page together, so compare with the month before.

Then decide

If readers visit, click and come back, add the next 50. If almost nobody visits, your readers prefer posts. Keep the shop small and put your effort there.

Questions people ask

From comments under shop tutorials and WordPress support forums.

Can readers buy without leaving my site?

No. The purchase happens at the shop you link to, and the click that takes the reader there is what credits the sale to you.

Can I copy product pictures and prices from Amazon?

No. Amazon's policies hand you its pictures through its API only, and forbid saving them on your server. Prices need the API or a link that Amazon serves, so a price you type yourself is out.

And two shorter ones

How many products can I import?

None of the sources we read names a fixed limit. Your hosting sets one, and long before that, the number of products you can honestly vouch for.

Can one page mix products from several shops and networks?

Yes. Every product keeps its own affiliate link, so each sale is credited through the right network.
