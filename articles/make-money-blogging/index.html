How to make money blogging in 2026.

A blog can still earn, and affiliate links can pay from the first sale. What works in 2026 is first-hand experience, readers of your own, and posts that help someone choose what to buy. Search alone is no longer a plan.

Is blogging still worth it in 2026?

The short answer.

Yes, if you know your subject from doing it. A commission can come with your first sale. A full income takes longer, and part 2 has the numbers.

In short

  • Affiliate links can earn from the first sale. They need no minimum traffic. Ads need traffic first, your own products readers who trust you, sponsored posts a loyal audience.
  • Many blogs earn their first dollar in the first year. The median was 12 months in a 2026 survey with 129 respondents, and about one in four got there within six.
  • Search sends fewer readers, and it is no longer the only way. Under an AI summary, people click a result about half as often, by Pew's measurement. About one in four bloggers in the survey named Pinterest as what helped their income most that year.
  • One good source of readers is enough to start. Pick the one that fits your subject, and start an email list so those readers stay yours.
  • It is not a way to a lot of money within months. Part 3 says who a blog fits.

Part 1 of 8

What changed for blogs since 2022?

Google answers more questions itself, and it tightened its rules for thin content.

If you have read that blogging is dead, this is where it comes from. For years a blog could live on one recipe: many how-to posts, visitors from Google, income from ads and links. Two changes broke it.

Google now answers many questions itself

Since 2024 Google has shown an AI-written summary, which it calls an AI Overview, above the results for many searches. A reader who gets the answer there has less reason to visit your post.

How much less is disputed.

Google tightened its rules

In March 2024 Google added spam policies against content produced at scale to win rankings, whether people or software write it. Its guidance on helpful content warns against pages that mainly summarise what others have said.

What happened to 100 blogs

In 2026 Daniel Stanica, who runs the site Monetize Better, went back to 100 blogs held up as success stories in 2022, and compared their estimated search traffic then and now.

The median blog had lost 85% of its search traffic. Twelve had none left. Twenty-one had grown.

What the blogs that grew have in common

Stanica names four traits. Every blog that held up had at least two.

  • Experience a summary cannot replace. A recipe you tested, a pattern you made, a product you used.
  • Readers of their own. An email list or a community, independent of Google.
  • A name people search for. Readers look for the blog itself.
  • A product of their own. Something readers buy from the blogger directly.

What 1,000 page views a month are worth

At 1,000 page views neither is an income yet. Both sums grow with your readers.

Who visits matters more than how many. The same traffic is worth more when readers are about to buy and the shop pays a higher rate.

Part 3 of 8

Is a blog the right tool for you?

It fits when you can show what you use. Three plans it does not fit.

A blog fits

When you can show, and let it grow

  • You know your subject from doing it, and can photograph or test what you write about.
  • You can let it grow beside what you do now. In the survey the median first dollar came after twelve months, and about one in four got there within six.
  • You would write about it anyway.
Look elsewhere

When one of these is your plan

  • You need a lot of money within months. Work for clients pays by the job, and a small blog can be your portfolio.
  • You plan to publish AI-written posts in bulk. Google's spam policies cover that.
  • Your plan rests on Google alone, in a hard-hit topic such as finance or health.

Which one first

Start with the one that fits the posts you already write. If they help people choose things to buy, that is affiliate links (part 5). If they answer questions for many readers, ads follow once the traffic is there.

A product of your own comes last. Respondents who mainly sell digital products reported the most per page view in the survey, but a product needs readers before it sells.

Part 5 of 8

Does affiliate marketing still work?

Yes, in posts that help someone decide what to buy.

In the 2026 survey, respondents whose main income is affiliate links reported about as much per 1,000 page views as those whose main income is ads: $34.66 against $33.16.

How it works

You recommend a product and link to it. A code in the link tells the shop the reader came from you. If the reader buys within the programme's time window, you are credited a share of the sale.

Each programme sets its own rate and window. Awin says most windows last 30 days; at Amazon a click counts for 24 hours.

Shops run these programmes through affiliate networks such as Awin, CJ or Rakuten; Amazon runs its own. To get a link, you sign up with a network, apply to a shop's programme and wait until the shop accepts you. The network then gives you your own link to that shop.

Before you publish an affiliate post

Mistakes that cost commissions

  • Linking where nobody is buying. Links earn in a comparison, a review or a gift list. In a post that explains a term, few readers are about to buy.
  • Never going back. Shops change prices, sell out and drop products, and a post that was right in March can lead to a dead end by June. More in why affiliate posts stop earning.
Every dead link is a commission you earned and never received.
  • Building on one programme. A shop can change its terms whenever it likes. Two or three programmes spread that risk.

Amazon in 2026. Amazon changed its programme terms on 14 April 2026. A sale that starts from an advert you paid for or boosted, a promoted pin for example, no longer earns a commission, with limited exceptions. Its published rates sit between 1% and 4.5% for most categories.

Where the link goes in your post

  • In a sentence. Enough for a passing mention.
  • On a photo. When a post starts from a picture, such as an outfit, a room or a desk, readers ask where each thing is from. By hand, a numbered list under the picture does the job.   • In a roundup or a review. Here the product is the point, so show it. Part 6 compares two ways.

Text links or product cards? The same picks, shown twice.

A text link never promised a price. A card does, so something has to keep it current.

Do cards earn more?

We found no independent study that compares the two. The tests that exist come from makers of affiliate plugins, and they measured clicks, not sales.

So test it: rebuild one post that already gets visitors, and compare a month of clicks before with a month after.

Two ways to make the cards

By hand. Each product needs a picture you are allowed to use, a typed price and your link. That works for a handful of posts. Every price change sends you back in.

From the shop's product feed. That is the shop's own list of its products, passed on to you by your affiliate network. A plugin reads it and you pick from it. If the plugin reads the list again on a schedule, price and link follow the shop.

Amazon has no feed: you add its products by link or ASIN from the Starter plan, and they show without picture or price. Picture and price need the Pro plan, once Amazon gives you access to its product data.

Part 7 of 8

How do you get readers to a blog now?

None of this earns without readers. Search, Pinterest and email each do a different job.

Search: still first, no longer enough

Write for search the way Google describes: make clear who wrote the post, how you know, and what you tested. Google says its AI summaries need nothing extra from you.

Rely on it less. Treat a visitor from search as a first meeting, and give them a reason to come back without Google.

Pinterest: visual and long-lived

Pinterest reported 640 million monthly users in mid-2026. Its help pages say a pin has no fixed lifespan and can gain attention months or years after you publish it.

Pinterest sends the reader; the earning happens on your blog. Affiliate links are allowed on pins as well, under conditions: Pinterest's affiliate rules in plain words.

Email: readers nobody can take away

An email list is the one audience that does not pass through somebody else's algorithm. Start it with your first post.

Part 8 of 8

What should you do in the first twelve months?

Five steps. The order matters more than the months. If you already have a blog, start at step 3, with the posts that still get visitors.

  1. Months 1 to 3: choose what you can show

    Pick a subject where you do the thing yourself: cook it, build it, wear it, test it. Write the first ten posts from that experience, with your own photos.

  2. From the first post: collect email addresses

    One sign-up box, and one short mail whenever you publish.

  3. Months 3 to 6: write what people read before they buy

    Comparisons, reviews of things you own, lists with a job, such as a packing list or a gift list. Join the affiliate programmes of two or three shops you already buy from. Add the links and the disclosure.

  4. Months 6 to 9: build up the source that sends readers

    See where your readers come from, and do more of that first. If your subject is visual and you are not on Pinterest yet, start there, with a pin for each post you have. If it has to be seen in motion, make a short video.

  5. Months 9 to 12: look at what was clicked

    Check your affiliate network's reports for what was clicked and what sold; the detail varies by network. Update those posts first, then write more like them. Once you pass about 1,000 sessions a month, apply to an ad network.

If month twelve ends with your first commissions, you are on the survey's usual path: the median first dollar came after twelve months, and each older group of blogs averaged more than the one before.

Questions people ask

How much traffic do you need to make money from a blog?

None to start, a lot to live on. Affiliate links can earn from the first sale. In the 2026 survey, blogs under 5,000 monthly page views averaged about $52 a month, and blogs with 25,000 to 49,999 about $990.

How many blog posts do I need before monetizing?

There is no number. You can add affiliate links to the first post that recommends something. In the survey, blogs under 100 posts averaged under $40 a month, blogs with 100 to 299 nearly $2,000.

Do affiliate links hurt SEO?

Not by themselves. Google's spam policies say that taking part in an affiliate programme does not make a site thin. Mark the links as paid and add your own view of the product.

How do you make money blogging with AI?

By using it as a helper. Google does not ban AI-written text, but its spam policies cover content produced at scale to win rankings. The part that earns is what software cannot supply: what happened when you used the thing yourself.

And two shorter ones

Do I need a blog for affiliate marketing?

No. Pinterest, for one, allows affiliate links directly on pins. A blog gives you a place you own, where one post can collect readers from search, Pinterest and email at once.

Do I have to pay taxes on my blog income?

That depends on where you live and how much you earn. Ask an accountant or your tax office before the first payout. This guide cannot advise you on it.
