How to make a holiday gift guide for your blog.

A gift guide you publish this autumn still has the December rush ahead: Pinterest says gift searches run from October to December, and searches for gifts for others surge in the final month. Here is how to build one readers can buy from right up to Christmas, with gifts still in stock at the price you show.

Questions people askWhere to go next

Is there still time for a gift guide?

Yes. Pinterest's UK team says more than half of its monthly users are likely to create festive wishlists they revisit later. A guide someone saves in October can get a second visit in December. The whole guide in five lines:

In short

  • Publish in October, pin until December. Make several pins, and keep the year out of the address.
  • Five to 15 gifts, sorted by person or budget. Each with one honest reason and a backup. Part 3 has the layout to copy.
  • Say that you earn, at the top. In plain words, before the first link, and on every pin that links straight to a shop.
  • Look daily from Black Friday to Cyber Monday. Prices move, deals end and gifts sell out.
  • Amazon gifts: no typed price, no copied picture. Amazon allows prices only in a link it serves, or from its API.

Part 1 of 7

When should you publish and pin it?

As early as you can. October is not late.

Pinterest's guide for brands asks them to go live “as early as possible between September and November”, and its UK team says people's own wishlists peak in November. A guide that is live in October is in place for both.

Two things to do before the first pin

  1. Check your words in Pinterest Trends. The free tool shows when interest in a term rises; Pinterest's team points people there for holiday timing.
  2. Make several pins, each with its own picture. Simple Pin Media advises little text and a concrete title. Yours could read “Gifts for the home cook under $50”.

Pinterest's own rules for affiliate pins are explained in our Pinterest guide.

Part 2 of 7

Which gifts go in, and how many?

About ten, and only ones you would recommend without the commission.

Five to 15 is the usual range; Matt McWilliams puts the sweet spot at eight to ten. One filler pick makes readers doubt the rest, he warns.

For your sections, type “christmas gift ideas” into Pinterest's search and look at the suggestions it shows: mom, coworker, teacher, best friend. Keep the people your readers buy for.

Then fill them from the shops you're already an affiliate for. Awin, an affiliate network, suggests asking a shop's affiliate team which products sell best in the season, and what is new.

Give every section a backup

Some gifts will sell out before Christmas. Note a second product for every section, at about the same price and from another shop if you can. A sold-out gift then costs you one swap.

Part 3 of 7

How should a Christmas gift guide look? Like a shop, not a link list.

Readers come to choose. Sort the gifts the way they shop: by who it is for, and by what it costs.

For price sections, WP Tasty suggests bands such as under $25 and $25 to $50.

The gift guide template, top to bottom

  1. A title that says who it is for.
  2. Your disclosure, before the first link (part 4).
  3. Two sentences on who the guide is for and how you chose.
  4. Jump links to each section, by person and by budget.
  5. Each gift with its name as a heading, a “best for” line and three to five sentences on why. Top pick first.
  6. A backup for every section.

The budget sections need a price with each gift, kept current. Parts 5 to 7 show how.

For pictures, use your own photos or the ones a shop gives its affiliates (a product feed carries one per product), not pictures cut from an image search or a shop's pages.

Part 4 of 7

How do you say that you earn from it?

At the top, in plain words, before the first link. A pin with an affiliate link needs its own.

In the US, the FTC warns that readers may not understand “affiliate link” to mean you are paid. One disclosure on your home page is not enough, and one at the bottom of a post is easy to miss.

In the UK, the ASA's most straightforward route is “Ad” in the post's title; where only some links in a post pay you, “(Ad)” before those links. On Pinterest, “Ad” at the start of the description is likely to be acceptable.

At the top of the post, before the first link

I get commissions for purchases made through links in this post.

In the title, for readers in the UK

Ad: Gifts for the friend who lives outdoors

Before a link that pays you, when only some do

(Ad)

At the start of a pin with an affiliate link

Ad: I earn a commission if you buy through this link.

On your site, clearly, if you link to Amazon

As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.

Wording to copy. The FTC's and Amazon's sentences are theirs, word for word; the others are examples. As of October 2026.

Elsewhere, look up your consumer authority: in Canada the Competition Bureau, in Australia the ACCC. The rules for pins are in our Pinterest guide.

Part 5 of 7

What about gifts from Amazon?

They are welcome in a gift guide. Amazon has its own rules for prices, pictures and deals.

The rules are in the Program Policies of Amazon Associates, Amazon's affiliate programme. For a gift guide, in our reading:

In your guide

Allowed

  • A text link with your own Associates tag.
  • Your own name for the gift, and your own reasons for it.
  • Price and availability, when Amazon serves the link that shows them, or they come from Amazon's API.
In your guide

Not allowed

  • A price you typed yourself. Amazon did not serve it, and it did not come from the API.
  • Amazon's product pictures saved, uploaded or edited into a collage.
  • Deal words such as “Black Friday deal” once the deal has ended.
  • A printed guide, or a PDF to print, with your Amazon links in it.

Amazon leaves one point open: whether a section called “Under $25” with Amazon gifts in it counts as showing a price. The safe way: Amazon gifts go in the sections by person, and the budget sections come from shops whose product feed gives you the current price.

Amazon's API, which delivers price and picture, opens only to Associates with ten qualifying sales in the past 30 days. Until then, a text link and your own words do the job.

Part 6 of 7

What changes in Black Friday week? Prices, deals and stock.

Prices move in the weeks around Black Friday. When the UK consumer group Which? checked the prices of 208 Black Friday deals from 2022 every day, only five were at their lowest on Black Friday itself.

What changes in a gift guide around Black Friday, and what you do about it
What changesWhat you do
A price drops or risesCorrect it. Write “on sale”, never “lowest price”.
A deal endsTake the deal words out that day. Amazon requires it for its own deals.
A gift sells outSwap in the section's backup.
A product is relisted or droppedReplace the link and check it shows the same product.
Shops announce new dealsAdd a Black Friday section, and take it down after Cyber Monday.

Deals arrive late. Awin notes that some shops share their Black Friday details only shortly before the day.

Part 7 of 7

How do you keep it right until Christmas?

Two full checks, a daily look in Black Friday week, and tools that take part of the work.

The goal: in December, readers find gifts that are in stock, at today's price, sorted by person and by budget.

Pinterest asks affiliates to check regularly for broken or outdated links. For a gift guide, plan two full checks, just before Black Friday and in early December. From Black Friday to Cyber Monday, add a quick daily look at prices, deals and stock.

Where it stops

  • Nightly, not instantly. A price changed at noon on Black Friday reaches the card after the next night, and a caching plugin can show an old price until its copy of the page is renewed.
  • Published posts only. Drafts and scheduled posts are not updated each night, so publish the guide rather than schedule it.
  • New and dropped products wait for the weekly read of the whole feed, or for a click on Reimport. Until then a dropped product keeps its last price.

Questions people ask

Asked under gift guide tutorials and in Pinterest's business community, or raised by the tutorials themselves.

Can I include gifts I haven't tried?

Yes, as long as you don't claim you have. The FTC's guidance is plain: you can't talk about your experience with a product you haven't tried.

Awin calls gift guides suggestions, not necessarily recommendations. Write them that way: “a good pick for a new runner”, and “I wear these every day” only where it is true.

Does Amazon allow gift guide collages?

Not made from Amazon's own pictures, in our reading. Amazon's licence says you may not store its product images, and its policies forbid changing its content. A collage does both.

If you have the gifts at hand, lay them out and take one photo of your own. With the Starter plan, MyFeeds' Shop the look puts numbered dots on such a photo and links each one to a product. An Amazon dot shows only the name you give it, your note and the link.

Can I offer a printable version with Amazon links?

Not with your Amazon links in it. Amazon's policies rule out promotion in any offline manner, and name printed material and ebooks.

For a printable list, leave the links out and point readers back to the post.

And four shorter ones

Should I use the same URL every year?

Yes. Keep one address and update the page each autumn. Put the year in the title if you like, never in the address: pins and links from past seasons then lead to this year's guide.

Does a gift guide work if I only work with one brand?

It can. Simple Pin Media's advice to a reader in that spot was to add products from other brands that go well with it, so readers still get a choice.

Can I put Amazon links straight on my pins?

Pinterest's team has said affiliate links can go straight into a pin's link field. Whether Amazon counts your Pinterest account as one of your sites, its public terms do not say. Our Pinterest guide has the details.

How do I see which gifts sell?

Simple Pin Media suggests a separate Amazon tracking ID for each gift guide, so its sales show apart from your other posts. For what to change next autumn, Google Search Console shows which searches brought readers to the guide.
