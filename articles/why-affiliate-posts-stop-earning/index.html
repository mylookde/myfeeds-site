Why old affiliate posts stop earning.

The readers still come, so the post can earn again. What changed is what happens after they click, and you can find it in an afternoon.

The short version

An old post that still gets readers has done the hard part. If it earns less than it did, look behind its links.

In short

  • A post with readers can earn again. The repair is in the links. The writing stays.
  • Three numbers say where to look. Visits, clicks on your links, commission.
  • Open your links the way a reader would. A sold-out product, a new price or a closed programme need not show any error.
  • Replace before you delete. A successor or an alternative keeps the page and its readers.
  • Start with your ten most-clicked posts. Plan about 90 minutes.

Part 1 of 9

Why does a post earn less when the readers still come?

Three numbers answer it. Read them for one post before you change anything.

“Earning less” can mean fewer readers, fewer clicks, or clicks that no longer pay. Each needs a different repair.

Compare the last three months with the same three months a year ago, so the season matches:

  • Visits, from your analytics.
  • Clicks on your affiliate links, from your network's reports or a tool that counts outgoing clicks.
  • Commission, from the network.
What the three numbers mean
What you seeWhat it means, and what to do next
Visits are downFewer readers arrive. That is about ranking or reach, a different repair from this one.
Visits hold, clicks are downReaders arrive and do not click. Open the post as a visitor: an empty product box or an outdated pick gives them nothing to click.
Clicks hold, commission is near zeroThe click ends somewhere that does not pay. Follow each link to its last address, as in part 3.
A reader asks

“A friend bought through my Amazon link and I got no commission. Is my link broken?”

Probably not. On Pinterest's business forum, where this was asked, another member named two likely reasons: the order had not shipped yet, and the buyer was someone the poster knows.

Amazon's policies speak to both: a commission needs an order that has shipped and been paid for, and you may not ask or encourage friends or relatives to buy through your links.

Give an order a few days to ship before you repair anything.

Part 2 of 9

What can go wrong behind a link?

Six things, and only some of them ever show an error.

Pages vanish all over the web. The Pew Research Center found in 2024 that 38% of the web pages that existed in 2013 could no longer be reached ten years later. That is the whole web. We know of no neutral count for affiliate links.

Old posts keep finding new readers all the same. Pinterest's help says a pin has no set engagement window and can gain engagement months or even years after it is first published. More in affiliate links on Pinterest.

Each link in such a post passes through three places, and only the first is yours.

What a closed programme looks like depends on the network. At Commission Factory the links break automatically, and pending commissions are still paid. Part 3 of 9

How do you find the posts that leak?

Ten posts, a private browser window and a spreadsheet. Plan about 90 minutes, and do it by hand once: you see what your readers have been seeing.

  1. Pick ten posts by clicks

    Sort by clicks on your affiliate links, not by visits: a dead link costs most where readers click most. Without click numbers, take the posts that used to earn most.

  2. Open each link in a private window

    It carries none of your logins or cookies, so you see what a first-time visitor sees.

  3. Read the last address

    Wait until the page has stopped redirecting. The address bar should show the shop's own domain and the page for that one product.

    Last addresses that mean trouble

    • The shop's home page, a category, a search page or an error page.
    • A page that says the link is inactive. On Awin, an address with closedMerchant.html means the shop's links are offline.

  4. Read the page

    Is it the product you wrote about? Can it be bought? Does the price match your post?

    Only look. Amazon, for one, does not allow orders through your own links.

  5. Check the programme

    In your network account, see whether you are still joined to the shop's programme.

  6. Write it down

    One row for each link. If more than a few no longer earn, give the next ten posts the same check.

Part 4 of 9

Why did my link checker find nothing? It looks for missing pages.

A checker asks one thing of every link: does this address return a page? A sold-out product and a new price sit on pages that do. So can a closed programme.

A blind spot

Redirects that fall home

If your links run through a short address on your own site, such as yourblog.com/go/shoes, a broken redirect can send every click to your home page.

One blogger's checker passed such links, because a home page loads.

False alarms

Working links, reported broken

In the support forum of one checker plugin, staff explain that some services treat its requests as bot traffic and turn them away, Amazon among them.

One user there counted 40 to 60 false reports a week.

A checker is still worth running for real errors. Treat its report as a list of hints.

Part 5 of 9

What do you do with each find?

Five questions, in this order. The first “no” tells you what to do. The post itself stays: it still has readers, and they came for a recommendation.

  1. Is the shop's programme still open to you?

    No: the link earns nothing, wherever it lands. Link the product at another shop you're an affiliate for, or take the link out.

  2. Is the product still sold?

    No: look for its successor. On Amazon a new edition gets a new product number, the ASIN, so the old link does not follow it.

    Without a successor, recommend the closest alternative and say why. If it is lighter or costs more, say that too.

  3. Does your link land on it?

    No: the shop moved the page. Get a fresh link to the product from your network.

  4. Can it be bought today?

    No, but it will be back: keep the link, add a dated note and name an alternative.

  5. Is the price in your post still right?

    No: take the number out, or say when you checked it. “See today's price at the shop” stays true whatever the shop does.

To copy: sold out, back soon

Sold out when I checked on DATE. The closest I would buy instead: PRODUCT.

To copy: a price you keep

PRICE when I checked on DATE. See today's price at the shop.

Not for Amazon products. Amazon's programme policies, as of April 2026, let your site show prices and availability only when Amazon supplies them, with the link or through its product API. For an Amazon product, type neither and name the alternative.

Part 6 of 9

Does a dead link hurt your ranking?

We found no statement from Google that it does. This is what is confirmed, what is opinion and what is open.

Written down by Google

Confirmed

  • Affiliate pages that only copy the shop's descriptions and reviews fall under “thin affiliation” in the spam policies.
  • Changing a page's date so it seems fresh, when the content has not substantially changed, is a warning sign in the guide to helpful content.
Said by practitioners

Opinion

Keep a post that has readers and repair it. For a product that is gone, forums advise swapping in a similar one.

Nobody has measured it.

Not settled

Open

Whether dead outgoing links lower a ranking. The spam policies do not mention them.

How much commission dead links cost. The figures in circulation come from sellers of link tools.

So the damage you can count on is lost commission and a reader's trust. And the thing to avoid is a new date on a post where nothing else is new.

Part 7 of 9

Why does it keep coming back? Your post holds a copy.

Whatever you repair goes out of date again, because the product in your post is a copy from the day you wrote it. The other way is to show it from the shop's own list, its product feed, and read that list again.

Part 8 of 9

How do you stop doing this by hand?

Four kinds of help. Each solves one part and is blind to another.

Change in one place

A link manager

  • One edit changes a link in every post that uses it.
  • Does not by itself notice that a link stopped earning.
Find missing pages

A link checker

  • Finds pages that return an error.
  • Blind to stock and price, and to an “inactive” page that loads.
Hear it early

Your network's messages

  • Can tell you that a programme is closing or moving before a reader does.
  • Easy to miss, and silent on one product's stock or price.
Read the shop's list again

A product feed

  • Price, stock, picture and link come from the shop's own list.
  • Only for shops that share a feed. Amazon does not.

Part 9 of 9

How often should you check?

One date in the calendar each quarter, and three triggers in between.

Nobody has measured the right interval. The advice in circulation comes from makers of link tools: at least quarterly (Lasso), or monthly for link checks (Geniuslink). Pick one you will keep.

Do not wait for the date when

  • Your network writes that a programme is closing or moving.
  • A shop relaunches its site.
  • A reader tells you a link is wrong. Check the rest of that post the same day.
The quarterly check, for your ten most-clicked posts

The ticks are only for you. Nothing is saved.

The last line is the one no tool can tick. It is why readers trust your picks.

What you should never have to do is hear it from a reader.

Questions people ask

From affiliate forums and WordPress support threads.

How do you check for broken Amazon affiliate links?

By opening each one in a private window and reading where it ends, as in part 3.

A general link checker is unreliable with Amazon: it can report working links as broken, as part 4 shows.

Can too many dead Amazon links get my account closed?

We found no rule that says so. In the forum threads where the claim appears it is hearsay, and other members doubt it.

What Amazon's policies do ask, as of April 2026, is that you promptly remove Amazon content that Amazon itself no longer displays.

And two shorter ones

Do affiliate links expire?

The link itself usually does not. What ends is what stands behind it: the product, the shop's programme, or your place in it. When ShareASale moved to Awin, short links without a click in the twelve months before stopped tracking.

My Amazon product boxes all say “no products found”. What happened?

Most likely the plugin behind them lost its connection to Amazon's product data. In one WordPress.org support thread that hit 132 of 133 pages, and the plugin's author pointed to missing access keys. Check the plugin's Amazon settings before you edit a post.
