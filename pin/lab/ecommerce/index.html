What stays buried without a shop

The shops you work with hand you their whole catalogue in a feed. On a blog, your readers meet almost none of it.

  1. Most of the catalogue stays hidden

    A single feed can hold thousands of products. Readers only reach the few in the article they landed on.

  2. Readers can read, but not browse

    No way to narrow things down by colour or price, no way to see what else you have. Someone ready to buy runs out of things to click.

  3. Shoppers search for pages you don't have

    People type a kind of product into Google, not the title of your article. Without a page for it, Google has nothing of yours to show.

The same products, as a shop

A page on your own site, at an address you choose. Switch between laptop, tablet and phone to see the same shop on each.

  1. They browse by category

    The categories you named, with more inside them: Men, then Jackets. Each one is a page with its own address.

  2. They narrow it down

    Filters like brand, colour, price and shop, each with the number of products behind it, so nobody clicks into an empty page.

  3. They search and sort

    One search box over everything you carry. Sort by newest, by price, or by what your readers click most.

  4. They buy through your link

    Every product goes out to the shop with your affiliate link. You never take a payment or send a parcel.

Behind the shop

You steer it from your WordPress admin.

Everything your readers meet is set up on a few screens, right next to your posts.

You sort products into categories

Name the categories your shop should have, and put categories inside them: Men can hold Jackets. A car parts site would say Brakes, Filters, Tyres.

Nothing moves without you. Put products in one by one, or let MyFeeds propose them by keyword, brand and feed and tick off what fits. Turn rules on, and products from later imports wait on a list for your approval.

Every category is a page with its own address, and its title and description for Google are yours to set.

A shortlist inside a category

Jackets holds every jacket you have. What you want to point at in October is a handful of them. A collection is that handful, with a name: Fall Jackets inside Jackets, in this example.

Collections sit in groups you name yourself, like Seasons or Fabrics. Each one is a page of its own, with its own address, your own words and a place in your sitemap, and it is a box in the filters, so readers can switch it on where they already are.

"Collections" is only the default word. Call them Looks, Edits or Drops, whatever fits what you write about.

Before you start the trial

The questions people ask about the shop.

What is the shop exactly?

A shop page on your own WordPress site. Products from the shops you're an affiliate for sit in categories, and visitors filter, search and sort them. Every buy happens at the shop through your affiliate link. You never take a payment or send a parcel.

Does this work with my affiliate network?

If your network gives you a shop's product feed link, MyFeeds reads it. AWIN, CJ, TradeDoubler, Impact, Rakuten, Webgains, Admitad, Pepperjam, Partnerize, FlexOffers and Sovrn are recognised out of the box, and other CSV, TSV, XML and JSON feeds work too. How to get a feed link from your network.

MyFeeds keeps being developed, and the aim is that everyone can connect the feeds they work with. If your platform isn't covered yet, we can connect it on request: write to business@myfeeds.site.

Do I need technical skills?

No code, no command line. If you can install a WordPress plugin and paste a link, you're set. The first time you open each screen, a short guided demo walks you through it.

Do I have to sort every product by hand?

No, and you are never forced to either. Put products into a category yourself, or let MyFeeds propose them by keyword, brand and feed and tick off what fits. Turn rules on, and later imports are proposed for the same categories, waiting on a list for your approval.

Does the shop change my blog?

No. The shop has its own address, which you choose, and your posts stay as they are. Both draw on the same products, so you keep one set, and readers reach them from an article or by browsing.

Can I make the shop look like my site?

Yes. Colours, fonts, products per row, the size and shape of the pictures, what a hover does, which side the filters are on and much more, with a live preview next to the controls.

What can I put in it?

Anything the shops you work with publish as a feed: clothing, tech, home, beauty, garden, hobbies, parts. If there is a product feed link for you to copy, you can build a shop from it.

What are collections, and do I need them?

A collection is a hand-picked shortlist inside one category, like Fall Jackets inside Jackets. It gets a page of its own and a box in the filters. They are on from the start, but a shop works perfectly well without a single one.

How long is the trial?

14 days with everything in it: the shop, the design editor, the SEO tools, the rules that sort your products and everything in Pro. The checkout asks for a card or PayPal to start it, and you can cancel from your dashboard before it ends.

What happens to my things if I cancel?

The shop page stops being reachable for visitors. Your products, feeds, posts and the free plugin keep working. Subscribing again brings the shop back the way you left it.
